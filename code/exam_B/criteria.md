# Exam B: what will count as the law failing (written 25 October 2026, after the truth was simulated and before anyone opened it)

The predictions in `predictions.json` were filed (SHA-256 `bc0b27f8c8268a6dd7f3e08f892073d515362cc01d80b9fe0f41f7e282047fd3`, 21:10 UTC on 25 October 2026) before the truth simulations were started at about 21:15 UTC. The truth file `truth_sealed_B.json` (SHA-256 `727b32b4ad4991aabd0520a0c210007d26c93a7f4898dda3872fc87e288cfea2`) was written at 21:28 UTC by a script that prints no values; the author of the law has not read it. These criteria were written after the simulations were run and before the file was opened, which is weaker than filing them before the simulations; the paper must say so.

A case fails if the law's prediction differs from the simulated value by more than 6 per cent (occupied volume fraction for G1 to G7, ratio of rippled to flat critical density for G8 to G10). The 6 per cent is the tolerance already used for the pre-registered ripple tests; the sampling error of 48 realisations in a box of 32 radii is of the order of 1 per cent. The exam as a whole is reported by its mean absolute error over the ten cases and by the list of failing cases; no composite score is used. G4 (log-normal width 0.6, above the largest width of 0.5 used in calibration) is an extrapolation and is reported separately, so that a failure there counts against the stated range of the dispersity term and not against the law inside its range. The aligned cases G5 to G7 use the mapping from the generator's director order to the law's local pair order that was found after the first exam; they are therefore a test of the mapping on new cases, and a failure there counts against the mapping.

If any of the law files named in `predictions.json` (`finallaw3.py`, `mylaw.py`, `align.py`, `spec.json`) differs from the hash filed there when the exam is scored, the law has changed and the predictions must be re-filed under a new hash; the truth, which does not depend on the law, can then be scored only against the new predictions with a note that the truth was simulated before they were written. If the simulator changes in a way that affects discs, cylinders, aligned generators or sheets, the truth must be simulated again.
