# The thin-plate offset: diagnostics of 25 October 2026 (night)

Author of the analysis: Claude (Anthropic), at the direction of the corresponding author. Script: `code/verification/offset_diagnostics.py`; output: `code/verification/offset_diagnostics.out`; new data: `fss_L192.json` and `fss_L128_extra.json` (run with `fss_big.py`, seeds 5192 and 5128, logs beside them). Nothing here changes a reported number in the paper; it narrows the list of explanations for the offset of the simulator's thin-plate thresholds from the published ones (discs 1.6 per cent, squares 1.7 per cent, triangles 2.9 per cent above Yi and Tawerghi 2009) and records what is still open.

## What was tested and what came out

The first question was whether the offset belongs to the estimator and not to the geometry. The simulator inserts particles one at a time into a periodic cube and records the number at the first non-zero winding, so the measured quantity is a mean over realisations of a first-passage count, whereas the published value is defined as the density at which half of all realisations of a Poisson ensemble span. Using the stored thresholds, the Poisson ensemble was constructed explicitly (the probability that a cube holding a Poisson number of particles with mean N has wrapped is the average over realisations of the Poisson probability that the number is at least that realisation's threshold). For boxes of 32 radii and above the weighted plateau is 0.9775 for the mean, 0.9786 for the median and 0.9784 for the Poisson fifty per cent crossing, which are 1.7 to 1.8 per cent above 0.9614. The estimator therefore moves the plateau by 0.1 to 0.2 per cent and does not account for the offset.

The second question was whether the extrapolation in box size hides a lower limit. The crossing point of the wrapping-probability curves of two box sizes needs no extrapolation, but the curves are nearly parallel at these sizes and the crossing is noisy; the six pairs tested lie between 0.970 and 0.980 with bootstrap errors of 0.003 to 0.009. Wrapping in a single direction, the periodic analogue of the spanning criterion of the published procedure, extrapolates to 0.9756 plus or minus 0.0012. Fits of the form eta(L) = eta_inf + a L^(-p) to the nine box means from 12 to 192 radii were made with eta_inf free and with eta_inf fixed at 0.9614. With the limit free the fit gives 0.9776 plus or minus 0.0004 with a very steep approach (chi-squared 4.4 on 6 degrees of freedom), so the means from 16 radii upward are flat within their errors. With the limit held at 0.9614 the quality of fit is kept (chi-squared 6.2 on 7) only if the exponent goes to zero and the amplitude to 0.017, which is a constant offset and not a finite-size trend. Before the further runs of this night, the same fit with the limit fixed gave an exponent of 0.07 and could not be excluded formally; after 60 more realisations at 128 radii (0.9783 plus or minus 0.0010, 0.9776 plus or minus 0.0007 pooled with the earlier 30) and 40 at 192 radii (0.9778 plus or minus 0.0009), a slow approach to 0.9614 is excluded in practice, because it would require the mean to fall by 1.7 per cent over a range of box sizes in which the means agree within 0.3 per cent.

The third question was whether tangent contacts are being decided wrongly. Scaling every disc radius by 1 plus or minus 10^-9 changed the first-wrap index in none of 40 configurations, so decisions at tangency do not matter at this level.

## What this leaves

The estimator, the finite-size extrapolation and tangent contacts are not the cause, and neither is a coding error in the simulator: the independent reimplementation of Level 3 reproduces the plateaus (discs 0.9752 plus or minus 0.0016) and agrees on every contact decision. What remains are explanations in the conventions that both programs share (sequential insertion into a periodic cube, the first non-zero winding in any direction, plates of equal area, reduced density defined with the plate area) and explanations on the side of the published value. Yi and Tawerghi placed plates in a unit cell with hard faces, counted a cluster spanning two opposite faces and extrapolated linearly in the reciprocal box side; the review (Level 4, finding 1) and the procedure test in Supplementary Note 2 of the paper show that reproducing it from its description moves the disc result by at most 0.4 per cent. Their data are not available to us. The paper therefore reports the offset as unexplained, the law's agreement with 0.9614 as true by construction, and every simulated threshold as divided by 1.015.

## For the human assessor

Two checks would be decisive and neither has been made. The first is whether the definition of reduced density, the box-side criterion and the first-passage count in `package/SPEC.md` are the ones the published value uses; this needs someone to read Yi and Tawerghi and the specification side by side. The second is an independent simulation of the thin-disc plateau by a person or a program that has not seen either specification, for example a standard cluster-spanning code for penetrable discs in a unit cell with a separate extrapolation, run to 192 radii.
