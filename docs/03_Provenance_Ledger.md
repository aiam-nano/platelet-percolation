# Provenance ledger for the calibrated law (finallaw3.py)

Version 2, 25 Oct 2026 (version 1 of 24 Oct is kept as 03_Provenance_Ledger_v1_24Oct.md). Purpose: for every term in the law, say where it came from, whether it was derived, taken from the literature, or fitted, and what data it was fitted to. A term that cannot be traced is marked as such. The law returns a nominal platelet volume fraction phi = n times the mean particle volume, with phi = base times dilution times polydispersity. The current implementation is finallaw3.py (constants in mylaw.py and at the top of finallaw3.py); finallaw2.py is kept because the sealed exam was first scored with it.

## Structure

phi = base(Ae) x dil(Ae, K) x P(sigma, Ae, kappa), where Ae is the effective aspect ratio A/N (A the aspect ratio of one layer, N the number of stacked layers), K = Kal(S, d) x Krip(a, lambda) is the anisotropy factor on the excluded volume, and P is the polydispersity factor. Three different things are called "volume fraction" in the project: the nominal fraction n times V (used in the law and in the benchmark), the occupied fraction 1 minus exp(minus n V) (used in the sealed exam), and the reduced density eta. Conversions between them are listed at the end.

## Terms taken from the literature or derived without fitting

The constant c0 = 1.4421 is 1.5 times the Yi and Tawerghi (2009) thin-disc reduced critical density 0.9614, since for a thin disc phi/eta = 3/(2A). It is a literature value. The simulator's own thin-disc plateau is 0.9767 plus or minus 0.0008 (boxes of 32 to 128 radii), 1.6 per cent higher; every simulated threshold used to fit or test the law is divided by 1.015 so that the simulator is on the Yi and Tawerghi scale. The law therefore reproduces the literature thin-disc limit by construction, and agreement with the simulator in that limit is not independent evidence. The plateau ratio measured on 25 Oct is 1.0159 plus or minus 0.0008 for discs; squares and triangles show offsets of 1.7 and 2.9 per cent over their published values, so 1.015 is a disc-specific number applied to everything (see 15_Shape_Ripple_FSS_Checks_25Oct.md).

The constants c1 = 2(pi + 3)/pi and c2 = 4/pi come from the Isihara mean excluded volume of a convex body, V_ex = 2V + S M / (2 pi), evaluated for a cylinder (volume pi R squared t, surface 2 pi R squared + 2 pi R t, mean-curvature integral pi t + pi squared R). The excluded-volume formula was checked by Monte Carlo (test T3, agreement within 1.5 standard errors at four thicknesses; for spheroids, literature_checks/spheroid_vex_test.py against 2V + S M / 2 pi within 0.1 per cent). Expansion of the ratio V_ex / V gives the denominator Ae + c1 + c2/Ae. These are derived, not fitted.

The elliptic-integral factor Kell(a), the arc-length ratio of a sinusoidal ripple of slope parameter a, is a closed-form geometric quantity and has no free constant.

The matrix (eigenvalue) treatment of polydispersity inside P, built from moments of a log-normal distribution, follows the standard second-virial argument and has no free constant of its own; it is exact only for log-normal radii and thicknesses.

## Fitted terms (all regenerated by calibrate_all.py; results in calibration_result.json)

B_ = 0.663 plus or minus 0.043 is the finite-thickness correction (1 + B_/(Ae + 1.5)) to the base. Fitted by weighted least squares to twelve monodisperse simulated points covering aspect ratios 5 to 500 (the list base in fitlaw.py), each divided by 1.015. Chi-squared 3.3 on 11 degrees of freedom.

kappa = 0.625 plus or minus 0.032 is the log-normal drift factor in P, (1 + kappa sigma squared). Fitted jointly to five thin-limit eigenvalue ratios and three finite-aspect simulations (eight points). Chi-squared 6.8 on 7.

The alignment factor is Kal(S, d) = 1 / (1 - c S (1 + gamma/(d/2) squared)) with the exponent 2 fixed. In version 1 the prefactor c was assumed to be 0.625 (see below) and gamma was fitted. In finallaw3.py both are fitted to the 24 simulated alignment ratios (aspect ratios 10, 30, 33 and 1e6; domain sizes 2 to 8 radii): c = 0.588 plus or minus 0.011 and gamma = 0.576 plus or minus 0.075, chi-squared 43.0 on 22 degrees of freedom (reduced 2.0). With c fixed at 0.625, gamma was 0.392 plus or minus 0.058 and chi-squared 66.7 on 23. The refit was made on 24 Oct after the sealed exam had been scored, and after the mapping of the exam's alignment parameters (below) had been made, so the aligned exam cases are not blind to it.

CRIP = 0.647 plus or minus 0.011 is the decay constant in Krip(a, lambda) = 1 + (Kell(a) - 1) exp(-CRIP a lambda). Fitted to 30 thin-limit paired ripple ratios (ripthin2_G.pkl); chi-squared 241 on 29. Version 1 of this ledger said "eleven simulated ripple points" in one place and 30 in another; 30 is what calibrate_all.py uses. Several functional forms for the ripple damping (a one-parameter exponential, a two-parameter stretched exponential, rational forms) were compared in ripfit variants before this one was chosen, so the number of forms tried exceeds the number of constants and the choice is a model selection that is not penalised in the quoted fit quality.

## Fit quality and cross-validation

The fit quality is not uniformly good. The alignment fit has a reduced chi-squared of 2.0 (free prefactor), and the ripple fit has a reduced chi-squared of 8.3, which means either that the one-parameter exponential form is inadequate or that the quoted simulation uncertainties are too small. The ripple term is therefore the least well established part of the law (in-sample RMS 3.1 per cent, worst held-out point 8.7 per cent). Leave-one-point-out cross-validation (verification/cv_loo.py, same weights) gave: B_ in-sample RMS 0.79 per cent and held-out 0.83; gamma with the prefactor fixed 1.55 and 1.68; prefactor and gamma both free 1.23 and 1.44; CRIP 3.06 and 3.21; kappa 1.69 and 1.76. This tests the stability of each constant, not the validity of the functional forms, which were selected by the author from several candidates.

## History of the alignment prefactor

The prefactor 0.625 was hard-coded in finallaw.py, finallaw2.py, fitlaw.py, revlaw.py and score7.py, and its derivation is not documented in any file. It coincides numerically with the fitted kappa, with a crossover value used in one of the AI-generated hypotheses, and possibly with an earlier fit; the author cannot establish which. The refit with the prefactor free is the current law. At the operating point of the exam (S_local about 0.24, d = 6) the two parametrisations differ by 0.7 per cent in K. Impact of adopting the refit (verification/prefactor_impact.py): the threshold changes by less than 1 per cent for local order up to 0.3 and domain size of 3 radii or more, by up to 2 per cent at the highest order considered (0.5) for domains of 6 to 8 radii, and by up to +4.5 per cent (aspect ratio 40) or +5.3 per cent (thin limit) for small domains of 2 radii at order 0.5. On the three aligned exam cases the error against truth falls from +1.7, +1.6 and +4.2 per cent to +1.1, +1.0 and +3.8 per cent.

## Count of constants against the goal text

Standard 2 asked for at most three adjustable fitted constants. The law now has five fitted constants (B_, kappa, the alignment prefactor, gamma, CRIP) and the benchmark-tied factor 1.015. The paper must not state that the law meets that preference without qualification.

## Operational definitions that the law depends on

The law's S is the mean second Legendre polynomial of the normal-vector angle between pairs of particles whose centres lie within two radii, not the order parameter about a global director. On the exam generator this equals 0.665 times the director order parameter squared, measured as 0.1662 plus or minus 0.0030 for S_dir = 0.5 and 0.2394 plus or minus 0.0030 for S_dir = 0.6. The law's d is the domain size in units of the radius, equal to 6 for the exam generator. These definitions were established after seeing exam truth (see 06_FinalLaw_Exam_Scoring.md), so the mapping from the generator's Fisher concentration to the law's S is post hoc.

## Unit conversions

The simulator returns n V in nominal units, divided by 1.015 for benchmark comparison. The exam asked for occupied fraction, so the law's nominal phi is converted by 1 minus exp(minus phi). The two differ by about 3 to 6 per cent at the volume fractions of the exam (0.01 to 0.12), which is larger than the law's error, so the conversion matters.

## Scope limits

The polydispersity factor is exact only for log-normal distributions; bimodal and thickness-radius-coupled distributions (exam cases F5, F6, F7) are outside the law, and two of the 24 pre-registered tests that probe such cases (B2 bimodal, H1 log-normal sigma 0.8) fail by +18.7 and +23.6 per cent. The ripple term has been tested only for single sheets in the thin limit; layered ripple stacks are untested. The law has no term for hard-core exclusion, tunnelling, or the effect of the polymer matrix.
