This is an independent reimplementation of the penetrable-particle percolation model in SPEC.md. The code is in work/core.py and work/run_all.py. Contact tests and the insertion loop are compiled with numba 0.68 on Python 3.14. Random numbers are numpy's PCG64 generator, one stream per realisation from SeedSequence([20261025, case index, realisation index]). The whole of step 3 and step 4 took 233 seconds. validate_results.py reported FORMAT OK. No realisation failed to wrap, and every raw realisation wrapped along x, y and z inside the insertion cap.

Thin discs use an exact chord test. The line where the two planes meet is built from the in-plane gradient of each plane, and the discs intersect when the two closed chords overlap. Exactly parallel planes fall back to a coplanar distance test. Finite cylinders and oblate spheroids use the Gilbert–Johnson–Keerthi algorithm with the analytic support function of the body, and they count as intersecting when the origin is within 1e-9 of the Minkowski difference. Square and triangular plates are clipped against each other's plane; parallel plates, including exactly coplanar ones, are reported as non-intersecting, which is the rule in section 2 of the specification. Particles are inserted in order into a cell list. Clusters are a union-find that stores the lattice offset of each particle relative to its root. A contact that joins two particles already in the same cluster is a wrap when that offset is inconsistent, and the edge is not unioned. N_any is the first insertion at which any winding component is nonzero, and N_x, N_y, N_z are the first insertions at which each component is nonzero. For the cases that store raw output the run continues until all three components have occurred. In this run that never went past the first wrapping insertion by enough to hit the cap: all three directions appeared inside the specified limit, so the means are means of the density at the first wrap. The reported mean is the mean of eta = (4 pi / 3) N / L^3 for discs, plates and spheroids, and of the nominal volume fraction phi = N <V> / L^3 for cylinders. The standard error uses the sample standard deviation with divisor n-1. The insertion cap is the greatest integer not above the expression in cases.json, so the 0.40 L^3 caps are 5529, 13107, 44236 and 104857.

The choices that the specification leaves open are written out in work/NOTES.md. The ones that affect numbers are the closed disc test (a shared boundary point counts), the 1e-9 GJK tolerance, the decision to keep inserting raw realisations until x, y and z have each wrapped, and the floor used on the insertion cap. Coplanar plates are non-contacts by the plate rule in section 2. The log-normal box of side 24 is not strictly larger than four times the bounding radius of a particle with radius near the cap of 6; those pairs still use only the nearest image. The measured average of P2(cos theta) for the Fisher distribution at kappa = 6.312 was 0.601 from the cosine formula and 0.601 from normals drawn about a director.

The self-tests are also in work/NOTES.md. The disc excluded volume was 9.932 ± 0.037 against pi squared. The cylinder excluded volume at radius 1 and thickness 0.5 was 20.303 ± 0.082 against 2V + SM/(2 pi) = 20.302. Square and triangular plates gave 10.981 ± 0.126 and 13.181 ± 0.194 against AP/2 = 11.137 and 12.693. The triangle sample is high by about two and a half standard errors; witnesses for the reported hits lie in both polygons, so the extra is consistent with Monte Carlo noise. Zero-thickness cylinders and very flat spheroids approach the disc predicate, and spheroids with equal axes reproduce the sphere test. A bug in the GJK witness, a zero weight times uninitialised memory, produced NaN witnesses on some sphere hits while the boolean stayed correct. The buffers were zeroed before the production run. After that, all 4000 deterministic cylinder pairs and all 4000 spheroid pairs certified: every hit has a point inside both bodies, and every miss has a separating direction whose gap matches the GJK distance. Disc, square and triangle pairs certified the same way, with zero failures. On twelve small random boxes the cell list agreed with an all-pairs search and with a 3×3×3 supercell wrapping test, including the insertion just before the wrap. A hand-built ring of seven discs wraps at insertion 7 and only along x.

Of the 4000 deterministic pairs, 495 discs, 1086 cylinders, 339 squares, 184 triangles and 835 spheroids intersect. All 20 configurations in each of the six sets wrapped; the N_any lists are in results.json. The case means, each with zero discards, are below.

The thin-disc reduced density moves only a little from L = 24 to L = 64, and the aspect-1000 spheroids sit on top of that same value. Squares sit lower than discs, and triangles lower than squares, which is the same ordering as the excluded volumes measured for those plates. Thicker cylinders reach a higher nominal volume fraction at the wrap than thinner ones. Nothing in the run was repeated or rescaled after the fact.

I am confident in the deterministic pair and configuration outputs, because the witness and separating-direction checks passed on every pair and the configuration grids matched an all-pairs search. I am confident that the case means come from that same contact code together with a wrapping rule that matched the supercell test on small boxes. The standard errors above are the statistical uncertainty of the means. The numerical caveat that remains is the 1e-9 GJK tolerance on near-tangent cylinders and spheroids; the certification bounds that to pairs whose gap is on that scale.
